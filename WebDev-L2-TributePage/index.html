01 THE JOURNEY

From a small town
to a national icon.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Growing up in a modest family, he developed an early interest in learning and science. His journey reminds us that a person's beginnings do not have to define the scale of their contribution.

After studying physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, he pursued aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. He went on to work with India's space and defence research programmes, becoming an important figure in the country's scientific development.

At the Indian Space Research Organisation, Kalam contributed to the development of launch vehicle technology, including the SLV-III programme that placed the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980. He later played a leading role in defence research and became widely known as the 'Missile Man of India'.

In 2002, he became the 11th President of India. Beyond public office, he remained deeply committed to education, meeting students and encouraging them to think creatively, work hard and contribute to society. He passed away on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture to students — a fitting reflection of his lifelong dedication to teaching.
